Monitoring and incident response

During the window, watch the things that mean somebody is losing money or trust. Ignore the things that only look dramatic.

Signals that matter

Launch monitoring, in priority order.
SignalSourceMeansResponse
State root disagreement between the two indexers Scheduled comparison at a common height Artifacts mean two different things Highest priority. Publish immediately, bisect, follow Report a disagreement.
Spike in SEED_COMMIT_TOO_YOUNG GET /patina/invalid-events People or a tool are miscounting the 144 block wait Publish a plain restatement. Check whether our own planner is at fault.
Spike in SEED_CARRIER_BELOW_MIN Invalid events Fees are coming out of the carrier somewhere Check the planner immediately. This one is probably our bug.
Spike in SEED_BAD_GRAMMAR Invalid events A builder is producing malformed markers Identify the pattern from the raw scripts, then publish the correct bytes.
Any invalid event traced to our own tooling Correlation with planner requests We cost a user real fees Disable the build helpers, fix, publish what happened and how many people it affected.
Sync gap growing status Answers are going stale Fail readiness on the lagging instance. See Incident actions.
Concentration climbing GET /patina/stats The founding cohort is being farmed Publish it. This is a known limit, not an incident, and hiding it would be the actual failure.
Deep or frequent reorgs Indexer reorg counter Recent artifacts are unsettled Label recent state as provisional. Do not hide it.
Error rate on the public API Edge metrics Readers cannot read Scale or rate limit. Never serve numbers without their height.

Signals that look alarming and are not

  • A slow mint rate. There is no target, no cap, and no race. A quiet day is not an incident and must never be treated as one in public.
  • An artifact resetting to Raw. Somebody spent their own coins.
  • A relic appearing. Also somebody spending their own coins.
  • A large single minter. Report it, do not react to it.
  • 503 during initial sync. That is readiness working correctly.

Incident response table

Severity, first action, and who decides.
SeverityExampleFirst actionDecision ownerPublic update
Critical State disagreement, or our tooling causing user loss Disable the affected lever, publish within the hour Protocol owner, with a second approver Immediately, then at every material change
High Indexer badly behind, node unreachable, storage corruption Fail readiness on the affected instance On call operator Within the hour if reads are affected
Medium Invalid event spike caused by third party tooling Read the transactions, identify the pattern On call operator with documentation owner A guidance post, not an incident notice
Low Latency, single instance restart, cosmetic bug Fix in the normal course On call operator In the regular update
Not an incident Slow minting, concentration, resets, relics Publish the measurement Nobody. It is normal operation. On the normal schedule

On call during the window

  1. Someone awake for the first 144 blocks after h_open, which is about a day.
  2. Named on call for the whole 4032 block window, with a documented handover.
  3. Cross indexer verification at least daily, logged with heights and roots.
  4. A second person able to approve a critical decision, because the critical actions require two.
  5. The escalation path written down before the window opens, not improvised during it.

What gets published, always

  • Any state disagreement, while it is unresolved.
  • Any bug in our tooling that cost anyone fees, with the number of people affected.
  • Concentration statistics, on schedule, whatever they say.
  • Corrections to anything we published that turned out to be wrong.

The rule is simple. If a fact would change how a reasonable person acts, publish it at the moment you know it, not when it is convenient.
